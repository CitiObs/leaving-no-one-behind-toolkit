# How can we maintain energy in our CO?

### **Why this is relevant**

Citizen observatories thrive when people feel energized—not just at the start, but over months and years. Research on citizen science participation reveals a consistent pattern: **initial recruitment is often successful, but retaining engaged contributors depends on factors many projects overlook.**

**What the evidence shows:**

* **Social connection sustains participation more than task interest alone.** Studies of long-running volunteer projects (20+ years) show that in-person interaction, recognition, and feeling part of a team predict retention far better than intrinsic environmental values. The risk to avoid is to prioritise data collection over relationship-building in the CO.
* **Motivation evolves.** People join for curiosity or environmental concern (intrinsic reasons), but stay for community, learning, and visible contribution. Projects designed only for initial motivation—clear mission statements but vague feedback loops—inadvertently alienate people who've evolved in their reasons for showing up.
* **Lack of feedback is actively demotivating.** When participants don't know if their data was used, if it changed anything, or how it mattered, they drift away. This is especially true for unpaid, voluntary work where the social return on effort becomes invisible.
* **Effort-support mismatch drives dropout.** Time commitment, unclear roles, non-intuitive tools, and lack of progression (doing the same task endlessly without learning or agency) consistently rank as top reasons people leave—not because they lost interest, but because energy drained faster than it was replenished.

**Take the time to assess what factors are at play in your own community**

Generic advice such as "build community" or "improve communication" is not terribly helpful without taking the time to understand the specific contours of your initiative and the motiviations of the participants. The Energy Diagnosis Chart is tool that can be used for discussion with team members and participants to map exactly where energy flows and where it gets stuck—and crucially, where interventions matter most. Is your bottleneck really about task difficulty, or is it poor feedback visibility masquerading as low interest? Are you losing people because they lack connection, or because leadership never rotates and new ideas don't get space?

**For inclusivity especially:** Energy imbalances often affect marginalized participants first. When roles are unclear or effort underestimated, people with fewer social anchors in the project tend to drop out before anyone notices. A shared energy diagnosis can surface these patterns early and help you redistribute effort and recognition equitably.
