# How to Host a Community Conversation

The [Interactivity Foundation](https://www.interactivityfoundation.org/) is a US-based non-profit organisation dedicated to fostering open, exploratory dialogue on shared public concerns. Their _How to Host a Community Conversation_ resource provides a concise, practical set of three interlocking guides — covering organisation, facilitation, and participation — designed for anyone wanting to bring a diverse group of people together for a structured yet open-ended conversation.

The guides are grounded in the principle that community conversations should be genuinely exploratory rather than focused on reaching a predetermined conclusion. They aim to expand the range of perspectives under consideration, encourage collaborative problem-solving, and ensure that diverse voices are heard. The facilitation guide in particular offers useful practical advice: aiming for 6–10 participants per facilitator, establishing clear participation guidelines, crafting a discussion outline with introduction, content development, and conclusion phases, and maintaining an exploratory rather than directive tone throughout. Together the three guides cover what each role — organiser, facilitator, and participant — brings to a successful community conversation, making them a useful starting point for citizen observatory teams planning their first community dialogue event.
