# How can we bring diverse stakeholders together in dialogue and deliberation?

## Why is it relevant?

Knowing that your citizen science initiative should be more inclusive is not the same as knowing how to make it so. One of the most powerful steps a practitioner can take is to deliberately bring together a diverse mix of community members, stakeholders, and experts in a structured space designed for open, multi-directional conversation. This is what dialogue and deliberation offer — and the two, while distinct, work in concert. Dialogue builds the trust, openness, and mutual understanding that makes it possible for people to listen deeply to perspectives different from their own; deliberation draws on that foundation to help participants closely examine options, share preferences, and move toward informed, collective decisions.

Unlike debates, which focus on points of disagreement and competing positions, or town-hall meetings, where community members have limited opportunity to speak and experts control the floor, dialogue and deliberation are cooperative rather than competitive. They create space to uncover common ground across diverse groups and allow multi-directional conversation between community members, policymakers, and practitioners — conversation that can surface assumptions, flatten traditional hierarchies, and make more room for those who experience systemic and institutional inequities.

Critically, this approach is grounded in the conviction that communities — diverse groups of people with shared priorities and interests — should have a say in matters that affect them, that they have valuable insights to offer, and that the work they do together to address a problem is indispensable. For citizen observatories, where the issues being investigated often disproportionately impact the most marginalised groups, the deliberative skills of identifying topics, designing inclusive events, facilitating well, and sustaining engagement over time are not peripheral concerns — they are foundational to achieving equitable outcomes.
